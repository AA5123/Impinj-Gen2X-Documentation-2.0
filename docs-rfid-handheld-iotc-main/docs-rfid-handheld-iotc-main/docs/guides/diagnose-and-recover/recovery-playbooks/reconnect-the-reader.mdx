Two playbooks for the case where the reader itself is the thing you cannot reach. RP-01 covers the local path — 123RFID Desktop over USB, the path you fall back to when no remote path survives. RP-07 covers the harder case: the broker still lists the reader as connected, but nothing is coming from it.

Run each step; verify the success check; move on.

### RP-01: Connect a Sled to 123RFID Desktop {#rp-01}

**When:** 123RFID Desktop cannot find your sled.

1. Confirm the USB-C cable is data-capable (not charge-only). Many cheap cables are charge-only; substitute a known-good cable.
2. Restart the reader, as its product guide describes.
3. Restart 123RFID Desktop.
4. **Success check:** the reader appears in 123RFID Desktop's list of readers with its serial number.

### RP-07: Diagnose Silent-Offline State {#rp-07}

**When:** Heartbeats stop arriving but the broker still shows the reader connected.

1. Send [`get_status`](/api-reference/mqtt/mgmt/get-status). If no reply arrives within your timeout, the reader is unreachable over this endpoint.
2. Check the connection-state topic for a `DISCONNECTED` `mqttConnEVT` you may have missed. Subscribe with the endpoint's actual topic; it is not retained reliably.
3. Check the broker's own view of the client: most brokers report when a client last sent anything.
4. Wait one and a half times the endpoint's `keepAlive` interval (the contract's binding uses 300 seconds). In MQTT 3.1.1 the broker then drops a silent client and publishes its Last Will, unless the client disconnected cleanly.
5. If the reader still does not respond, restart it at the reader, as its product guide describes.
6. **Success check:** heartbeats resume, and a `CONNECTED` `mqttConnEVT` arrives.
