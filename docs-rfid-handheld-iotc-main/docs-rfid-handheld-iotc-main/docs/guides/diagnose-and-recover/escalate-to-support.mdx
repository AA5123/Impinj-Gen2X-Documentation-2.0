A support case without evidence takes a round trip before anyone can act on it: the first
reply is a request for the things below. Capture them while the symptom is still
reproducible and the multi-day thread collapses into a single exchange.

## What to Capture

| Evidence | Where it comes from | Why it shortens the round trip |
|---|---|---|
| **Reader model and serial** | RFD40 or RFD90, and the serial from the device panel | Says which sled you have and which physical unit the case is about |
| **Firmware version**, the exact string | [`get_version`](/api-reference/mqtt/mgmt/get-version) | The exact build decides whether the behavior you saw is expected |
| **Reader state while the symptom is happening** | [`get_status`](/api-reference/mqtt/mgmt/get-status) | Any reply at all proves the broker path works; `radioActivity`, `powerSource` and `batteryStatus` then say what the reader reported about itself at that moment |
| **The failing command's exact published payload**, including the `requestId` you used | Your client, verbatim — not retyped from memory | `requestId` is the only way to pair a reply with its request; without it a reply cannot be tied to the request that caused it |
| **The reply, verbatim**, including `response.code` and `description` | Your client's subscription. See [Response codes](/api-reference/mqtt/response-codes) | The code names the failure class, which is what determines whether the fault is yours, the reader's, or a precondition you had not met |
| **The MQTT topic** the message was published to or received on | Your endpoint and subscription configuration | Separates "the reader never got it" from "the reader answered somewhere you were not listening" |
| **`mqttConnEVT` and `alerts` events** from a window around the failure | Your event stream | A `CONNECTED` that is minutes old with nothing since is a different fault from an alternating CONNECTED / DISCONNECTED stream |
| **What you expected, and what happened instead** | You | Distinguishes a defect from a configuration or expectation mismatch before anyone reproduces anything |

## Capture It in This Order

Order matters, because the later items are only meaningful once you know which reader and
which firmware produced them.

1. The reader's serial number and the [`get_version`](/api-reference/mqtt/mgmt/get-version) response.
2. A [`get_status`](/api-reference/mqtt/mgmt/get-status) response taken **while the symptom is
   happening** — a status taken after a reboot describes a reader that no longer has the problem.
3. The failing command's exact published payload and the `requestId` you used.
4. The reply you received, verbatim, including `response.code` and `description`.
5. The `mqttConnEVT` and `alerts` events from a window around the failure.
6. The MQTT topic involved, and a one-line statement of what you expected instead.

:::caution[Redact before you attach]

Screenshots and log extracts routinely carry a Wi-Fi SSID, a device MAC address,
internal IP addresses, an endpoint hostname with a tenant identifier, or a device
serial. Remove anything you would not publish before attaching it to a public thread.

:::

## Then Open the Case

With the evidence in hand, [Get Help](/resources/support/get-help/) has the routes: the
self-service surface to try first, the reference page that is the authority on the command
you called, and the Zebra channels for a case, a download, or a question to the community.
