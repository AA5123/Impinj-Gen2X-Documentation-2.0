MQTT moves messages between the reader, the broker and your application, but it does not tell your application what happened on the reader. This page explains what the MQTT API guarantees, what it leaves to you, and what an application can and cannot conclude when messages go missing.

## Commands Can Arrive More than Once {#at-least-once}

Commands and replies travel at QoS 1. In MQTT 3.1.1, QoS 1 delivers a message at least once, so the reader can receive the same command twice. The reader keeps no request history and suppresses nothing: it executes every copy, whatever its `requestId`.

What a repeat does depends on the command:

| Class | Commands | A repeat |
|---|---|---|
| Safe to send again | Every `get_*` command | Returns the state again and changes nothing |
| Refused while the first is in effect | `set_wifi`, `config_endpoint`, `set_os`, `reboot`, `set_operating_mode`, `control_operation` | Is refused with a status code, such as `18` for a repeated `set_wifi` `CREATE` or `4` for a repeated `set_os` |
| Not documented | `delete_wifi_profile`, `install_certificate`, `delete_certificate`, `config_events`, `set_post_filter` | Is most likely executed again. Treat it as a second real request |

For a configuration command, the last copy to arrive takes effect. A refusal after a retry is information, not failure: it can mean that your first request succeeded. See [Retry Commands Safely](/guides/build-your-integration/retry-commands-safely).

The reader queues at most 10 commands. Commands beyond that are not queued, so an application that sends a large batch at once can lose the overflow rather than have it delayed.

## Replies Are Unordered, and Nothing Times Out {#replies}

Replies arrive out of order and interleaved with events, so the `requestId` is the only thing that pairs a reply with its request. The contract specifies no timeout for any command. Choose your own, and decide what your application does when it expires: a missing reply does not tell you whether the reader acted.

## Subscribe Before You Publish {#subscribe-first}

Replies, events and tag data are not retained. The broker delivers each one only to the clients subscribed at that moment. Anything published before your subscription is active is lost and is not delivered again. This matters most for the asynchronous commands, `set_os` and `install_certificate`, whose outcome arrives as an event after the reply.

## Some Messages Are Lossy by Design {#lossy}

| Message | QoS | Retained |
|---|---|---|
| Commands and replies | 1 | No |
| `alerts` | 1 | No |
| `heartBeatEVT` | 0 | No |
| `mqttConnEVT` | 0 | Not settled: the channel declares it not retained and the operation declares it retained |
| `dataEVT` | Configurable per topic; 0 by default | No |

At QoS 0, the MQTT layer does not resend a message lost in transit. `dataEVT` is published at the QoS set on its topic, 0 or 1, and the reader's default endpoint configurations use 0. It is not retained; the contract describes the stream as a statistical view of the tag population, not a guaranteed log of every read. See [The Tag Data Stream](/guides/core-concepts/the-tag-data-stream#statistical-view).

## When the Connection Drops {#disconnects}

Each endpoint's `mqttParams` controls how its session behaves:

- **`keepAlive`** is the keep-alive interval in seconds. In MQTT 3.1.1, the broker treats a client as gone when it hears nothing from it for one and a half times this interval.
- **`reconnectDelayMin`** and **`reconnectDelayMax`**, in seconds, bound how long the reader waits before reconnecting after a connection is lost. The minimum must not exceed the maximum. The contract does not describe the back-off between the two.
- **`cleanSession`** defaults to `true`. In MQTT 3.1.1, a clean session discards any stored session when the connection closes. With `false`, the broker can keep QoS 1 commands sent while the reader was offline, but only with a stable, non-empty `clientId`.

When the reader connects, it registers a Last Will with the broker: the `DISCONNECTED` form of `mqttConnEVT`, on the endpoint's connection-state topic. The reader publishes a `CONNECTED` `mqttConnEVT` when its session is established. If the session ends without a clean disconnect, the broker publishes the Last Will, without a timestamp. A clean disconnect suppresses the Will, and the contract does not say whether the reader reports one. The absence of a `DISCONNECTED` message therefore does not mean that the reader is connected.

The contract describes no store-and-forward: nothing in it says that the reader keeps events or tag data while it is offline and sends them later.

## What You Can Know {#what-you-can-know}

An application never knows directly what a reader is doing. It receives signals, and it draws conclusions from them. Keeping these layers apart prevents most monitoring mistakes:

| Layer | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Reality | What the reader is actually doing | The reader is running an inventory |
| Signal | What the reader or the broker publishes | `heartBeatEVT`, `mqttConnEVT`, the Last Will |
| Observation | What your application received, and when | "The last `heartBeatEVT` arrived at 10:14" |
| Inference | What your application concludes | "The reader was active at 10:14" |
| Decision | What your application chooses to do: its policy | "Raise an alert after three missed intervals" |

- **A signal sent is not a signal received.** A QoS 0 message can be lost, and a subscription can start too late to see one.
- **Infer, do not assume.** An application never knows that a reader is online; it infers it from recent evidence.
- **Show evidence and its age, not verdicts.** For example: "Last `CONNECTED` 10:02 · last `heartBeatEVT` 10:14 · last `get_status` 10:15".
- **Decisions are your policy.** How many missed heartbeats count as an outage is your choice, not a property of the reader.

The reader publishes `heartBeatEVT` at the configured interval (at most 65,535 seconds) while the heartbeat is enabled with `config_events`; a reboot applies the setting. Monitoring can rely on it as its primary liveness signal: when `heartBeatEVT` stops arriving, the reader, its session, or the path to your application needs attention. See [Monitor Reader Connectivity](/guides/operate-and-monitor/monitor-reader-connectivity).
