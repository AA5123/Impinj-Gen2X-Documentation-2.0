Connections drop: Wi-Fi fades, brokers restart, applications are redeployed. This page shows how to make your application recover cleanly. To get a connection back that stays down, see [Network and Connectivity](/guides/diagnose-and-recover/diagnose-by-system-area/network-and-connectivity) and [MQTT Connection](/guides/diagnose-and-recover/diagnose-by-system-area/mqtt-connection).

## What the Reader Does {#reader-side}

When a reader's connection to the broker is lost:

- **It reconnects on its own.** Each endpoint's `reconnectDelayMin` and `reconnectDelayMax`, in seconds, bound how long it waits between attempts. The contract does not describe the back-off between the two.
- **The broker may publish its Last Will.** If the session ended without a clean disconnect, the broker publishes the `DISCONNECTED` form of `mqttConnEVT`, without a timestamp, on the endpoint's connection-state topic. A clean disconnect suppresses it, and the contract does not say whether the reader reports one.
- **It publishes `CONNECTED` when it is back.** A `CONNECTED` `mqttConnEVT` shows that a new session was established.

The contract describes no store-and-forward. Nothing in it says the reader keeps events or tag data while it is disconnected and sends them later, so plan as if anything published during an outage is lost.

With `cleanSession: false` and a stable, non-empty `clientId`, the broker can keep QoS 1 commands sent to the reader while it was offline, in MQTT 3.1.1 terms. The reader's endpoints default to `cleanSession: true`.

## What Is Lost {#what-is-lost}

| Message | During an outage |
|---|---|
| `dataEVT` | Lost. Published at the QoS set on its topic, 0 or 1, and not retained |
| `heartBeatEVT` | Lost. Published at QoS 0 and not retained |
| `alerts` | Lost if it could not reach the broker, or if your application was not subscribed |
| Replies to commands | Lost if your application was not subscribed when they were published |
| Commands to the reader | Lost unless the broker holds them for the reader's session |

## Configure Your Application's Session {#application-session}

1. **Subscribe on every connect.** Put your subscriptions in the connect callback, so they are renewed after every reconnect. With a clean session, the broker forgets them when you disconnect.
2. **Subscribe with a QoS at least as high as the message's.** A subscription at QoS 0 downgrades a QoS 1 `alerts` event.
3. **Use a persistent session if you must not miss QoS 1 messages** while your application is down: `cleanSession: false` with a fixed client ID. How long the broker keeps the session depends on the broker; see its documentation.

## Recover After Your Application Reconnects {#after-reconnect}

1. **Settle the requests that were pending.** For each command in your pending map, you do not know whether it reached the reader, whether the reader acted, or whether its reply was lost. Do not resend it blindly: read the state back with the matching `get_*` command first. See [Retry Commands Safely](/guides/build-your-integration/retry-commands-safely).
2. **Re-read the reader's state.** Send [`get_status`](/api-reference/mqtt/mgmt/get-status), and the `get_*` commands for the domains you depend on, such as [`get_endpoint_config`](/api-reference/mqtt/mgmt/get-endpoint-config) and [`get_operating_mode`](/api-reference/mqtt/ctrl/get-operating-mode). Replace your cached view with what they return.
3. **Treat the gap as unknown.** Record the time your application was disconnected, and mark any view of the reader from that period as missing evidence rather than as "no change".

## Recover After the Reader Reconnects {#after-reader-reconnect}

When a `CONNECTED` `mqttConnEVT` arrives for a reader:

1. **Refresh your model of the reader** with `get_status` and the other reads above. A reconnect may follow a restart, and a restart resets the operating mode to its default. `CONNECTED` alone does not show whether the reader restarted, because a network reconnect produces the same message.
2. **Re-apply the operating mode** if the reader restarted, then restart an inventory if one should be running.
3. **Reconcile tag data** if you need it. See [Reconcile When You Need To](/guides/build-your-integration/consume-tag-data#reconcile).

Watch `heartBeatEVT` as well: it keeps arriving at its interval while the reader, its session and the path to your application are working. See [Monitor Reader Connectivity](/guides/operate-and-monitor/monitor-reader-connectivity).
